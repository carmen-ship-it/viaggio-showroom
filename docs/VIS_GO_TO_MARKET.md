# VIS Go-To-Market Strategy

Dealership software sales strategy for VIS.

Audience: founders, sales leaders, dealership operators, implementation
consultants, automotive software investors, and channel partners.

Goal: win the first paying customer, prove measurable dealership value, convert
pilot usage into recurring software revenue, expand through dealership groups,
expand to other GAC markets, and eventually sell beyond GAC.

## GTM position

VIS should not be sold as a kiosk, website, dashboard, or AI experiment.

VIS should be sold as:

> A showroom conversion system that helps dealerships capture, route, and prove
> high-intent buyer demand from in-store vehicle exploration.

The sales promise must be practical:

- More visible showroom intent.
- Faster response to hot visitors.
- Better test-drive and financing handoff.
- Consistent vehicle education.
- Better attribution from showroom engagement to sales activity.

Do not promise guaranteed vehicle sales. VIS influences sales outcomes, but the
dealer controls inventory, pricing, financing, staff behavior, and closing.

## 1. First paying customer

### Target first customer

The first paying customer should be Viaggio.

Viaggio is the right first customer because:

- The current product is already tailored to Viaggio and GAC.
- Viaggio has the strongest incentive to prove the product.
- Viaggio can provide product, sales, and operational feedback.
- Viaggio can become the reference customer for other GAC dealerships.
- A Viaggio case study can support importer or dealer-group expansion.

### First customer offer

Sell Viaggio a paid founder pilot, not a free proof-of-concept.

Recommended commercial structure:

- Setup / implementation fee: USD 8,000 to 18,000.
- Monthly SaaS and support: USD 2,000 to 4,000.
- Minimum pilot commitment: three months.
- One rooftop.
- One approved vehicle content pack.
- Up to two customer devices.
- Up to ten staff users.
- Weekly performance review.
- Reference rights conditional on agreed success metrics.

### Why not free

Free pilots are dangerous because:

- Staff treats the product as optional.
- Management does not enforce process.
- Scope expands without commercial discipline.
- Renewal conversations become harder.
- No willingness-to-pay is proven.

If Viaggio will not pay at least a founder-pilot fee, VIS has not yet proven
urgent business value.

## 2. Pilot sales process

### Sales objective

The pilot sale should answer one question:

> Will this dealership pay to prove that VIS can create and recover measurable
> sales opportunities?

### Sales cycle

Expected sales cycle for founder pilot:

- Short founder-led cycle if Viaggio decision makers are aligned.
- Longer cycle if legal, GAC/importer approval, hardware, or CRM requirements
  become prerequisites.

Do not allow the pilot sale to become an enterprise procurement project.

### Pilot sales stages

#### Stage 1: Executive problem alignment

Buyer conversation:

- What happens when showroom visitors browse but do not talk to staff?
- How fast are test-drive and WhatsApp leads contacted today?
- Which GAC objections slow down sales?
- How does management know staff followed up?
- What does Viaggio need to prove before expanding?

Required output:

- Written problem statement.
- Named executive sponsor.
- Named sales operations owner.

#### Stage 2: Commercial pilot proposal

Proposal should include:

- Pilot scope.
- Included devices/users/vehicle pack.
- Implementation responsibilities.
- Pricing.
- Success metrics.
- Reporting cadence.
- Data requirements.
- Exclusions.
- Pilot-to-customer conversion terms.

Do not sell vague innovation. Sell a measured dealership workflow.

#### Stage 3: Operational readiness

Confirm:

- Staff owner.
- WhatsApp process.
- Test-drive scheduling process.
- Financing handoff process.
- Content approval owner.
- Device readiness.
- Reporting owner.
- Outcome-entry process.

If the dealership cannot name owners, the pilot should not start.

#### Stage 4: Launch

Launch activities:

- Configure dealership and vehicle.
- Approve content and disclaimers.
- Train staff.
- Register devices.
- Run lead capture test.
- Run staff handoff test.
- Confirm export/reporting process.

#### Stage 5: Weekly value review

Weekly review should focus on:

- Sessions.
- Leads.
- Lead response times.
- Test-drive requests.
- Scheduled and completed drives.
- Financing-interest leads.
- Staff usage.
- Outcome completeness.
- Missed handoffs.
- Operational blockers.

Avoid vanity metrics such as time spent, page views, or "wow factor" unless
they connect to lead actions.

### Pilot stakeholders

Required stakeholders:

- Dealer principal / owner / general manager.
- Sales director.
- Floor manager.
- Sales consultants.
- Finance manager.
- Marketing manager.
- CRM/process owner.
- Implementation lead.

Optional stakeholders:

- GAC/importer representative.
- IT/network support.
- Legal/compliance contact.
- Device/hardware vendor.

### Pilot objections and responses

| Objection | Response |
| --- | --- |
| "We already have a CRM." | VIS is pre-CRM. It captures showroom intent and sends richer leads into CRM. |
| "Our salespeople already talk to customers." | VIS measures whether hot buyers are actually captured, contacted, and followed up. |
| "We do not want another dashboard." | Version 1 is a workflow: capture, claim, contact, schedule, outcome. Reporting is only to prove results. |
| "Can we do a free pilot?" | No. A paid pilot ensures management attention and proves commercial seriousness. |
| "Will this guarantee more sales?" | No software can guarantee sales. VIS proves leading indicators and attribution that can drive more sales if staff follows the process. |
| "Can it integrate with our CRM now?" | Start with export or one-way integration. Deep integration is paid scope after lead value is proven. |
| "Who owns content accuracy?" | Dealer/OEM approves all claims, pricing, warranty, financing, and media before launch. |
| "Will staff use it?" | Pilot requires a named manager to enforce usage and review weekly metrics. |

## 3. Conversion from pilot to software customer

### Conversion goal

The pilot should convert into a paid software subscription if VIS proves
operational value.

Conversion should not wait for perfect sales attribution. Automotive sales
cycles and inventory constraints can delay final vehicle sales. The conversion
case should combine leading indicators, staff adoption, and early sales outcomes.

### Pilot conversion criteria

Recommended conversion thresholds:

- Lead capture process operating reliably.
- Staff claims hot leads within agreed SLA.
- Test-drive requests are captured and followed up.
- Outcome recording reaches acceptable completeness.
- Management can identify missed or recovered opportunities.
- At least some leads can be connected to test drives, financing conversations,
  or sales pipeline.
- Sales leadership agrees VIS improved process visibility.

### Conversion offer

After pilot, offer one of:

#### Standard rooftop subscription

- Setup already paid; charge transition/setup only for added scope.
- Monthly SaaS: USD 4,500 to 8,500 per rooftop.
- One-year agreement preferred.
- Includes standard support, reporting, and ongoing configuration.

#### Expanded Viaggio package

- Add vehicles, devices, or locations.
- Monthly SaaS increases by rooftop/device/vehicle scope.
- Additional vehicle content pack fee applies.

#### GAC rollout package

- Convert pilot results into importer/dealer-group proposal.
- Include Viaggio reference data.
- Use standardized content and rollout approach.

### Pilot conversion sales motion

1. Present pilot scorecard.
2. Show operational before/after.
3. Highlight missed opportunities VIS made visible.
4. Quantify test-drive and financing pipeline.
5. Show staff response behavior.
6. Propose standard subscription and expansion scope.
7. Secure annual commitment or multi-rooftop rollout path.

### What blocks conversion

- Staff did not use workflow.
- Outcomes were not recorded.
- No executive sponsor attended reviews.
- Dealer expected guaranteed sales.
- Product was treated as a demo kiosk.
- CRM integration became a blocker.
- Content/legal approval delayed launch.
- Inventory/pricing issues made GAC sales hard to attribute.

## 4. Expansion strategy inside a dealership group

### Expansion logic

Do not sell a dealership group on abstract platform features first. Sell them on
operating consistency across rooftops.

Group-level pain:

- Different rooftops follow up differently.
- Staff product knowledge varies.
- Management lacks comparable lead process data.
- Marketing spend is not tied to showroom outcomes.
- Model launch quality varies.
- CRM data quality differs by location.

VIS group value:

- Standard vehicle education.
- Standard lead capture.
- Standard handoff workflow.
- Location-level accountability.
- Group-level reporting.
- Shared content packs with local overrides.

### Land-and-expand sequence

1. Win one flagship rooftop.
2. Prove lead capture and staff workflow.
3. Add one additional rooftop as controlled replication.
4. Compare rooftop performance.
5. Package group rollout.
6. Standardize training and content.
7. Add CRM/export integration where volume justifies it.

### Group stakeholders

- Dealer group owner / CEO.
- Group COO.
- Group sales director.
- Group marketing director.
- CFO.
- IT/CRM owner.
- Rooftop general managers.
- Brand/importer representative if applicable.

### Group expansion pricing

Recommended:

- Group setup: USD 45,000 to 120,000.
- Per-rooftop activation: USD 8,000 to 25,000.
- Monthly group minimum: USD 12,000 to 25,000.
- Per-rooftop SaaS: USD 3,000 to 6,500 after minimum.
- Additional content packs and integrations priced separately.

Discounts should be tied to signed rollout commitments, not verbal expansion
promises.

### Group objections

| Objection | Response |
| --- | --- |
| "Every rooftop operates differently." | VIS supports local configuration while standardizing the core lead workflow. |
| "Managers will not change process." | Rollout requires group COO sponsorship and rooftop accountability. |
| "We need all CRMs integrated first." | Start with export and one prioritized connector. Integration depth follows adoption. |
| "We need custom reporting." | Begin with standard conversion metrics; custom reporting is paid scope after data quality is proven. |
| "Our brands have different needs." | Use shared platform workflow with brand-specific content packs. |

## 5. Expansion strategy to other GAC markets

### Why GAC is the strongest adjacent expansion

GAC markets likely share the same core commercial problem:

- Brand trust needs to be built.
- Product knowledge must be consistent.
- Customers compare against more familiar brands.
- Dealer networks need launch enablement.
- Financing, warranty, service, and resale concerns matter.

VIS can be positioned as GAC dealer enablement, not just dealership software.

### Best buyer

The best buyer may be:

- Country importer.
- Regional distributor.
- Dealer group representing GAC.
- OEM regional team.

Selling rooftop by rooftop is slower. Selling through importer/distributor
channels can create faster network adoption if Viaggio proof is strong.

### GAC market entry package

Offer:

- GAC model launch content foundation.
- Country localization.
- Dealer configuration.
- Lead workflow.
- Staff training.
- Reporting.
- CRM/export baseline.
- Reference case from Viaggio.

### Sales cycle

Expected stages:

1. Viaggio case study and executive introduction.
2. Importer/distributor discovery.
3. Market localization assessment.
4. Pilot rooftop selection.
5. Commercial proposal.
6. Content/legal approval.
7. Pilot launch.
8. Network rollout decision.

### GAC market stakeholders

- Importer/distributor CEO or commercial director.
- National sales manager.
- Brand manager.
- Dealer development manager.
- Marketing lead.
- Aftersales/service representative.
- Legal/compliance reviewer.
- Pilot dealership owner/GM.
- CRM/IT owner.

### GAC market objections

| Objection | Response |
| --- | --- |
| "Our market is different." | VIS localizes country, language, warranty, financing, and dealer process while reusing the core workflow. |
| "We already give dealers brochures and training." | VIS turns training into customer-facing conversion and measurable lead workflow. |
| "Dealers may not adopt it." | Start with one committed pilot rooftop and use adoption metrics before rollout. |
| "We need OEM approval." | Use brand-approved content pack process and signed claim approvals. |
| "We cannot share customer PII with importer." | Importer reporting can be aggregate and PII-free. |

## 6. Expansion strategy beyond GAC

### When to expand beyond GAC

Do not expand beyond GAC until VIS has:

- One paid deployment.
- One renewal or expansion.
- Repeatable implementation package.
- Proven lead workflow.
- Reference data.
- Content pack process.
- CRM/export baseline.

Without this, beyond-GAC expansion becomes unfocused custom sales.

### Best beyond-GAC target segment

Target challenger or trust-sensitive automotive brands, not all dealerships.

Best fit:

- New entrant brands.
- Chinese OEM brands.
- EV brands.
- Premium-but-less-known brands.
- Markets where customers need education before purchase.
- Dealer groups launching unfamiliar models.

Poor early fit:

- High-volume legacy brands with mature demand.
- Dealers only seeking generic CRM.
- Dealers with low showroom traffic.
- Dealers unwilling to enforce process.
- Used-car-only operations unless separately validated.

### Beyond-GAC positioning

Position VIS as:

> A conversion system for dealerships selling vehicles that require education,
> trust-building, and consistent follow-up before customers commit.

Avoid positioning as:

- Generic dealership software.
- CRM replacement.
- Website vendor.
- Kiosk vendor.

### Beyond-GAC sales motion

1. Use GAC/Viaggio proof to show category.
2. Target brands with similar trust and education barriers.
3. Sell one paid pilot per brand/dealer group.
4. Package content and workflow.
5. Expand through brand/importer channels.

## Pricing model

### Pricing structure

VIS should use:

1. Setup / implementation fee.
2. Monthly SaaS per rooftop.
3. Content pack fees.
4. Integration fees.
5. Support tier fees.

### Recommended pricing

| Package | Setup | Monthly SaaS |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Founder pilot | USD 8,000 to 18,000 | USD 2,000 to 4,000 |
| Standard rooftop | USD 18,000 to 40,000 | USD 4,500 to 8,500 |
| Dealer group | USD 45,000 to 120,000 | USD 12,000 to 25,000 group minimum plus USD 3,000 to 6,500 per rooftop |
| Additional vehicle content pack | USD 5,000 to 15,000 | USD 500 to 1,500 |
| Custom CRM integration | USD 20,000 to 100,000 | Support fee or integration maintenance fee |

### Pricing rules

- Never include unlimited customization.
- Never bundle deep CRM/DMS integration into base SaaS.
- Never make success fees the primary model.
- Do not price only by user seat.
- Discounts require signed term, rollout volume, or reference rights.
- Hardware is pass-through or separately managed.

## Objections

### Business objections

| Objection | Sales response |
| --- | --- |
| "We do not need this." | Ask how many showroom visitors leave without a captured lead, test drive, or follow-up. |
| "This is just a kiosk." | VIS is the workflow behind the kiosk: lead capture, handoff, follow-up, and reporting. |
| "It is too expensive." | Compare monthly cost to two to five incremental vehicle gross profits or recovered missed opportunities. |
| "We cannot prove it sells cars." | Start with leading indicators and attribution; do not rely only on final sales. |
| "Our staff will not use it." | Then do not buy it. VIS requires management enforcement to work. |

### Technical objections

| Objection | Sales response |
| --- | --- |
| "It must integrate with our CRM." | Version 1 supports export first; deeper integration is paid once lead value is proven. |
| "Our data cannot leave the CRM." | CRM remains customer/pipeline system of record; VIS stores showroom session and consented lead context. |
| "We need SSO." | SSO is enterprise scope; pilot and standard deployments can use scoped staff access first. |
| "We need custom dashboards." | Standard reporting proves conversion; custom reporting comes after data quality and workflow adoption. |

### Operational objections

| Objection | Sales response |
| --- | --- |
| "Consultants already follow up." | VIS measures response time and outcome completeness instead of relying on assumption. |
| "Customers prefer talking to people." | VIS does not replace people; it tells staff who is ready and what they care about. |
| "We do not have time to train staff." | Without training and enforcement, the pilot should not start. |
| "Our showroom traffic is low." | VIS may not be the right first investment unless marketing can drive traffic. |

## ROI proof requirements

### Minimum proof for pilot conversion

VIS must prove:

- Leads are being captured.
- Staff is responding faster.
- Test-drive or financing intent is visible.
- Management can see outcomes.
- At least some opportunities are attributable to VIS.

### Required pilot metrics

- Sessions started.
- Leads captured.
- Lead capture rate.
- Advisor requests.
- Median advisor response time.
- Test-drive requests.
- Scheduled test drives.
- Completed test drives.
- Financing-interest leads.
- Contacted leads.
- Won/lost outcomes where available.
- Lost reasons.
- Staff adoption.

### ROI calculation

Use conservative logic:

```text
Monthly VIS cost
  / average gross profit per vehicle
  = incremental vehicle sales needed for break-even
```

Example:

- Monthly VIS cost: USD 5,500.
- Average gross profit: USD 2,500.
- Break-even: about 3 incremental vehicle sales per month.

But do not rely only on sales count. Also show recovered process value:

- Missed handoffs avoided.
- Faster follow-up.
- More test drives scheduled.
- Better lead source attribution.
- Cleaner lost-reason data.

### Proof standards

Acceptable proof:

- VIS lead matched to CRM record.
- VIS lead matched to phone/WhatsApp follow-up.
- Test-drive status entered by staff.
- Closed won/lost manually entered or imported.
- Management review confirms operational impact.

Weak proof:

- Page views.
- Demo compliments.
- Time on kiosk.
- Staff anecdotes without lead records.
- Unverified sales claims.

## Reference customer strategy

### Viaggio as reference customer

Viaggio should be positioned as the founding reference customer if:

- Pilot is paid.
- Staff uses workflow.
- Results are measurable.
- Viaggio approves public or semi-public case study language.
- GAC/importer approves brand claims if included.

### Reference assets

Create:

- One-page case study.
- Pilot scorecard.
- Before/after workflow diagram.
- Quote from GM or sales director.
- Screenshots with no customer PII.
- Short sales deck for GAC markets.
- Implementation checklist summary.

### Reference claims to pursue

Safe claims:

- "Captured X showroom leads."
- "Reduced median handoff response time to X."
- "Generated X test-drive requests."
- "Improved visibility into financing-interest leads."
- "Standardized GAC product education."

Avoid unless fully proven:

- "Increased sales by X%" without clean attribution.
- "Guaranteed ROI."
- "Automated sales process."
- "Replaced CRM."

### Reference use in expansion

Use Viaggio proof for:

- Other GAC dealerships.
- GAC importer/distributor conversations.
- Dealer group expansion.
- Challenger-brand sales conversations.

Do not overuse Viaggio proof for unrelated dealership categories until beyond-GAC
fit is validated.

## Final sales guidance

The VIS sales motion should stay brutally practical.

Sell:

- Captured leads.
- Faster response.
- Test-drive conversion.
- Financing handoff.
- Staff accountability.
- GAC trust-building.
- Dealer group consistency.

Do not sell:

- A futuristic AI platform.
- A dashboard suite.
- A CRM replacement.
- A free pilot.
- A custom software project hidden inside SaaS pricing.

The first GTM milestone is not market domination. It is a paid Viaggio pilot
that produces enough evidence for a dealership operator or importer to say:

> This made buyer intent visible, improved follow-up discipline, and is worth
> rolling out to more rooftops.
